## Overview

This case study is based on a gym business that manages its members,
fitness classes, trainers, equipment, and workout tracking. The system
is designed to help gym staff manage day-to-day operations and allow
members to track their fitness progress over time.

The business problem this database solves is the need to replace
manual paper-based records with a structured digital system that can
handle member registrations, class bookings, trainer assignments,
equipment tracking, and payment records efficiently.

---

## Business Context

**Business Name:** FitLife Gym
**Type of Business:** Fitness centre offering memberships, personal
training, and group fitness classes.

**Key Operations:**
- Members sign up for monthly or yearly membership plans
- Members can book group fitness classes (e.g. yoga, HIIT, cycling)
- Trainers are assigned to members for personal training sessions
- Members log their daily workouts and track progress over time
- The gym tracks equipment for maintenance purposes
- Payments are recorded for membership fees and personal training

---

## Why This Case Study?

This case study was chosen because:

1. It involves multiple real-world entities with clear relationships
2. It covers a range of database concepts including one-to-many and
   many-to-many relationships
3. It is realistic, practical and relatable
4. It provides enough complexity to demonstrate normalisation,
   query optimisation, transactions and NoSQL modelling
5. It is different from the Library example provided in the codelab

---

## Scope

The database will cover the following areas:

- Member registration and profile management
- Membership plan management (Basic, Standard, Premium)
- Trainer profiles and assignments
- Group fitness class scheduling and bookings
- Personal training session records
- Daily workout and fitness progress logs
- Equipment inventory and maintenance tracking
- Payment and billing records
